## 1. Audit errors worth correcting

The grok reviews accept "pincer", "strictly stronger" and "closes the gap". The sonnet reviews are right on the logic, with some slips:

- **Direction of implication.** Lemma 4.1 makes unit and counit 1-outcome, so a right adjoint on Instr maps 1-outcome maps to 1-outcome maps. The hom-bijections then restrict to Chan, so no adjoint in Chan ⇒ none in Instr, not the reverse. sonnet1 §1.1 ("neither implies nor excludes") is wrong here, and the grok reviews miss it. This also contradicts the Intro's motivation that richer outcomes "might allow" an adjoint, since they only add constraints.
- **sonnet1 §5.2** has the right conclusion for the wrong reason. Without Lemma 4.1 the slices still correspond, with n ↔ nm. The prime step is idle because the intercept argument works for any m.
- **sonnet3's triangle-identity shortcut** makes the counit deterministic only at objects of the form A⊗E. It says nothing about ε_B when d_E ∤ d_B.
- **sonnet2 §D** is too harsh on "intercept scaling multiplicatively". The wording is loose but not wrong: the intercept is −d_source², and the source goes A → A⊗E.
- **grok2's** only Diophantine example (d_B = 1) is trivial. The relevant family is (d_B, d_E, d_R) = (k, 2k, 2k²−1), since d_E²(d_B²−1)+1 = (2k²−1)². Examples are (2,4,7) and (4,8,31). A search found 70 solutions with d_E < 400 and d_B < 60.
- **grok3** says the n=1,2 proof "recovers the Diophantine obstruction as the n=1 shadow". It doesn't: n=1 alone is satisfiable, and the contradiction comes from n=2 (or from choosing B = E, see 2B.1). Its measurable-outcome extension fails as stated, because for infinite outcome sets |I×M| = |I| and divisibility disappears. Infinite affine dimensions also can't mismatch, so the mechanism is intrinsically finite-outcome.
- **grok1 and grok3** call §5 "appropriately caveated". Its opening disclaimer is contradicted by "Theorem 4.2 proves/reveals…" in §5.1–5.2, and the abstract and §7 state its content as established.

## 2. Remaining flaws in the manuscript

### A. Foundations (repairable; the theorem survives)

1. **Instr isn't a category as defined.** With outcome sets as data, I×{∗} ≠ I, so the unit law fails and hom-collections are proper classes. Remark 2.1 is also self-contradictory. The minimal fix is outcome sets [n] with lexicographic product. That gives a genuine category with a strict F_E, and Thm 4.2's proof goes through verbatim because it only uses F_E as an endofunctor.
2. **Cor. 4.4 needs more.** The tensor of instruments is never defined. On the skeleton the interchange law holds only up to a permutation of outcomes, so ⊗ isn't a bifunctor. There are two fixes:
   - Define instruments as finite multisets of CP maps, which is strictly monoidal up to the Hilbert-space associator, and replace affine dimension by semialgebraic dimension. That dimension is invariant under finite quotients and semialgebraic bijections, and Φ⁻¹ is one.
   - State the corollary for F_E only.
3. **Missing lemma: affine bijection ⇒ equal affine dimension.** An affine map that is injective on a convex set C with nonempty relative interior is injective on aff(C), because a kernel direction would move an interior point inside C. The strictly positive point in Prop. 3.1 is exactly what this uses, and the text should say so.
4. **Domain conditions.** Prop. 3.1 fails at d_B = 0: it gives a negative number for an empty set. The proof also applies it to R(B) without showing R(B) ≠ 0. That follows because Instr(A⊗E,B) ≠ ∅ while Instr(A,0) = ∅ for A ≠ 0. Require d ≥ 1 throughout.
5. **Naturality.** Φ⁻¹(g) = ε_B∘F_E(g) is the standard consequence of naturality, and the text should say so. It matters because any two non-singleton convex sets with nonempty interior are in set-bijection. The theorem is only about natural, hence affine, bijections.
6. **Lemma 4.1 is idle.** Φ⁻¹ maps slice n bijectively onto slice nm, and the two-equation argument gives d_E² = 1 for any m.
7. **Notation.**
   - Φ means both the bijection and a CPTP map.
   - E means both the environment and the CP maps E_i.
   - I means the instrument, the outcome set and the identity operator.
   - B means both an object and B(H).
   - O(ε_B) and dim_aff are undefined.
   - Fig. 1's "Instr(A)" clashes with hom notation.
   - The Schrödinger-picture and Choi conventions go unstated.

### B. Logical position of the theorem

1. **"Strictly stronger… closes the gap" is false.** The paper never proves the Chan result it claims to extend. Chan closes in three lines. Take A = ℂ, B = E, n = 1. Then d_R² = d_E⁴ − d_E² + 1, which lies strictly between (d_E²−1)² and (d_E²)², so it is never a square. A search to d_E = 300,000 found none, and the sandwich is an identity. The missed point is the quantifier: R must exist for every B, while the n=1 count is satisfiable only for special (B,E).
2. **The only defensible "strengthening" is pointwise, and the paper never says it.**
   - In Chan, Chan(−⊗E, ℂ) is trivially representable, since every hom-set is a singleton.
   - In Instr, A = B = ℂ already fails. n-outcome POVMs on E have dimension d_E²(n−1), and n-outcome ensembles on R have dimension n·d_R²−1. With counit outcome count m the slices correspond n ↔ nm, so n·d_R²−1 = d_E²(nm−1) for all n, and n=1 and n=2 together force d_E² = 1. A search found no solutions for any m.
3. **"n=1 shadow" and §4.3's "connects directly to the deterministic one"** invert what the computation shows. n=1 is consistent, and n=2 is what contradicts it.
4. **"Pincer", "independent", "continuous".** The arguments are sequential in the text, one is redundant in logic, and n is an integer.
5. **The opfibration is never constructed** (Cor. 4.3, Fig. 1). The paper also calls F_E both the "transition functor" and the "cocartesian lift". A lift is a morphism over a base arrow, and F_E would be the reindexing functor u_!. A construction that works:
   - Take a poset P with a functorial assignment u ↦ E_u (E_id = ℂ, E_{vu} ≅ E_u⊗E_v).
   - This gives a pseudofunctor P → Cat, X ↦ Instr, u ↦ F_{E_u}.
   - Its Grothendieck construction is an opfibration, and it is a fibration iff every F_{E_u} has a right adjoint. By Thm 4.2 that holds iff every E_u is 1-dimensional.
   - Redraw Fig. 1 with u, u_! and the missing u*. This also gives "Past/Future" a meaning.
6. **Cor. 4.4.** Closedness is a property of the category, so one E suffices and "for any non-trivial environment dimension" is confused. Also state the iff: F_E has a right adjoint ⇔ d_E = 1, since the converse is just F_E ≅ Id.

### C. Interpretation (§1, §5, §7)

1. **F_E is not open-system dynamics.** It appends a spectator and carries natural maps tr_E and id⊗ρ with tr_E∘(id⊗ρ) = id. A real open-system evolution needs a state of E, an interaction and a partial trace, and none appear. "Forward evolution is canonical" is just uniqueness of cocartesian lifts.
2. **A right adjoint is currying.** The counit R(B)⊗E → B is evaluation and points forward. Petz [2] and Bayesian inversion [3] are prior-dependent daggers, and no link to adjoints is derived. [4] is about indefinite causal order, not retrodiction.
3. **Three cheap tests show that non-existence of R is not a signature of irreversibility, quantumness or time direction.**
   - *Reversible case.* In the groupoid of Hilbert spaces and unitaries, F_E has no right adjoint whenever d_E ∤ d_B, because the left hom-set is empty. Conversely, CPM(FHilb) is compact closed (Selinger, the paper's uncited ref. 7) and has E*⊗− despite containing the trace.
   - *Classical case.* For stochastic instruments the dimension is |X|(n|Y|−1), the same intercept argument applies, and it gives |E| = 1. Nothing quantum is used.
   - *Time symmetry.* F_E has no left adjoint either. In Chan, ℂ is terminal but F_E(ℂ) = E is not. In Instr, the same grading argument applied to the unit makes the n=1 slices correspond: a single point (the trace) against a continuum of channels X → E. Passing to Instr^op (Heisenberg picture) just swaps the two statements.
4. **Irreversibility is partly definitional.** Composition multiplies outcome counts, so every n>1 instrument has no inverse, including the coin flip {½id, ½id}. That says records can't be erased. It is not dynamical irreversibility, and coarse-graining would destroy the grading.
5. **Prior, apparatus and outcome set are conflated.** Retrodiction is functorial once the prior is part of the object (pointed channels; Cho–Jacobs, Parzygnat et al., so verify before citing). "Formal signature of apparatus-dependence" is unsupported.
6. **Smaller points.**
   - "Entropy increase on the base poset" and "time-symmetric poset" are not meaningful.
   - "Most general post-measurement update" is false even within scope (continuous outcomes in refs. 5–6, supermaps).
   - §5.2's "applies to measurement-induced irreversibility" contradicts §5.3.

### D. §6

- The undone items below refute that claim:

The claim that it "enacts" the boundary fails because the abstract and §7 state §5 as results. The "intellectual integrity" sentences carry no content.

## 3. Upgrades, by value per effort

1. **Make the pointwise witness (2B.2) the main theorem.** Drop Lemma 4.1 and the primes, and state the iff with d_E = 1.
2. **Add the Chan theorem (B = E)** and the reduction "adjoint on Instr ⇒ adjoint on Chan". Say plainly that the pointwise Instr statement is the independent content.
3. **Add no-left-adjoint and the classical analogue.** Each is short, and together they dismantle the left/right-asymmetry narrative honestly.
4. **Supply the explanation the paper lacks: the obstruction is normalization.**
   - In CPM, Hom(A⊗E,B) and Hom(A,E*⊗B) are PSD cones of the same dimension d_A²d_E²d_B².
   - TP costs d_A²d_E² constraints on the left but only d_A² on the right. They differ by d_A²(d_E²−1), which is Prop. 3.1's intercept. For d_A, d_E, d_B = 2, 2, 3 the TP slices have dimension 128 versus 140.
   - The asymmetry behind this is causality. In Chan, ℂ is terminal but not initial for d ≥ 1, so Chan ≄ Chan^op. That is the defensible categorical "arrow" candidate, and it is a property of the category, not of F_E.
5. **Replace the retrodiction story with a theorem that is about retrodiction.**
   - A channel N has a left-inverse channel iff N(ρ) = V(ρ⊗σ)V† for an isometry V and fixed ancilla state σ.
   - Equivalently, its complementary channel is constant (Knill–Laflamme with the whole space as code).
   - This ties irreversibility to what E learns and needs no adjoints. Graded versions (Petz recovery fidelity, or D(ρ‖σ) − D(Nρ‖Nσ)) quantify it, and Petz's sufficiency theorem [2] is the right citation.
6. **Reformulate positively.** The would-be R(B) = [E,B] is the convex set Chan(E,B), a comb-type object and not a quantum system. Closure holds in the higher-order completions. "The internal hom is not a quantum system" is the precise form of the title claim.
7. **Replace §6** with a short scope-and-limits remark. It should cover the hypotheses used (finite outcomes, rigid grading, TP normalization, finite dimension), counter-models where each is dropped, and what isn't claimed. Or delete it.
8. **Open problems worth a paper.**
   - Does non-closure survive the physically natural category where proportional outcomes are merged? The grading collapses there. I'd guess yes, via normalization, but that is a guess I haven't checked.
   - The infinite-dimensional and measurable-outcome case needs a convex-geometric invariant, because dimension counting is vacuous.
   - Prove a representability statement that doesn't rely on dimension counts. Dimension counting can't distinguish Chan(E,B) from a state space when d_E²(d_B²−1)+1 is a square. Classically it never distinguishes them at n=1: a product of simplices has |B|^{|E|} vertices against |E|(|B|−1)+1.
