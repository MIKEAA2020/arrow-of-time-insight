## 0. Overall assessment

The mathematical core is a short dimension count. I checked it and it is essentially correct, given the definitions as written. Almost everything built on top of it is either not derived, overstated, or inconsistent with the paper's own statements:

- the "pincer" and "prime argument" framing
- the claim to strengthen the deterministic result and close its gap
- the opfibration and bifibration language
- the retrodiction and arrow-of-time interpretation
- the "epistemic asymptote"

Details follow, roughly in order of severity.

---

## 1. Claims about the deterministic result and the "closed gap" (p. 1, abstract, §4.3, §7)

**1.1 "Strictly stronger than the deterministic one and closes the earlier mathematical gap completely" (Intro) is unsupported.**
- Chan is a wide subcategory of Instr, and a right adjoint to −⊗E in one category neither implies nor excludes one in the other.
- The theorem says nothing about Chan.
- The gap the author admits is that the n = 1 equation d²_R = d²_E(d²_B−1)+1 has integer solutions. A nontrivial one is d_E = 8, d_B = 4, d_R = 31, since 64·15+1 = 961 = 31².
- For such parameters the n = 1 dimension count gives no contradiction, and this paper adds nothing for Chan.
- The deterministic obstruction (the headline of [1]) therefore remains unproven, and the abstract's "extend … from deterministic channels" is a different theorem about a different category.

**1.2 "This gap … does not weaken the physical conclusion" (Intro) is a non sequitur.**
- A proof with a false lemma leaves the conclusion unestablished.
- "Physical conclusion" is also not defined at that point.

**1.3 §4.3 says the two-equation proof "connects the instrument obstruction directly to the deterministic one."**
- It does the opposite. It shows that the n = 1 (deterministic) equation alone is insufficient, and that n = 2 is what produces the contradiction.
- It also shows the theorem needs only two slices, so "∀n ≥ 1" in §4.2 is excess.

**1.4 "The deterministic obstruction is only the n = 1 shadow of a deeper obstruction" is a metaphor, not a result.**
- The paper itself shows that the n = 1 slice does not exhibit the obstruction.

---

## 2. Category definition (§2)

**2.1 The outcome set is "part of the data" (Def. 2.2), and the hom-set is a disjoint union over n ≥ 1.**
- Outcome sets range over all finite sets, so Instr is not locally small as written.
- The grading by n is only well defined after a choice of representatives. This is never done.

**2.2 Remark 2.1 ("Strictification") is self-contradictory and unproven.**
- "Strictly associative only up to canonical relabellings" is a contradiction in terms.
- (I×J)×K ≠ I×(J×K) as sets, so Def. 2.2 does not give a strictly associative category.
- The remedy is to identify instruments up to relabeling of outcomes. Then Instr_n is a quotient of a set of n-tuples by S_n, not a convex set of tuples in a vector space.
- The assertion that "outcome cardinalities and affine dimensions are invariant under this equivalence" is made without proof. The affine-bijection step in Thm 4.2 is stated for the tuple model, so the remark does not rescue it.

**2.3 Monoidal structure is never defined.**
- The tensor of two instruments (outcome set I×J) and the unit object are not specified.
- Yet Remark 2.1 speaks of "the tensor product" and Cor. 4.4 asserts non-closedness. Only F_E = −⊗id_E is defined.

**2.4 Zero-dimensional space.** "Objects all finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces" includes dim 0, where Prop. 3.1 gives 0 and "cancel d²_A" is illegal. A ≠ 0 must be chosen.

**2.5 Notation clashes and undefined symbols.**
- Φ denotes both the adjunction bijection and a CPTP map in Lemma 4.1's proof.
- O(ε_B) is undefined.
- Instr(A) in Fig. 1 and §1 conflicts with Instr(A,B) as a hom-set.
- "Past/Future" with "causal order" is never defined.

**2.6 The result depends on a modeling choice that is never discussed.**
- Outcome labels are rigid and cardinality multiplies under composition, with no coarse-graining or merging of outcomes.
- The p-outcome instrument {(1/p)Φ}_{i≤p} is operationally just Φ with a dummy random label. The category nevertheless treats it as a distinct morphism, and Lemma 4.1 is entirely outcome-count arithmetic.
- Any operationally natural identification (merging equal or zero components, coarse-graining) destroys the grading, and the proof with it.
- So the "physical" force of the theorem is largely a bookkeeping artifact. This should be stated as a limitation.

## 3. Proposition 3.1

The formula d²_A(n d²_B − 1) is correct. The Choi constraint count is right, the surjectivity onto Herm(A) is right, and J_i = I/(n d_B) gives Σ Tr_B J_i = I_A as required. This is a fine argument. The strictly positive interior point is the right justification.

## 4. Lemma 4.1 ("prime argument")

**4.1 The prime argument is redundant.**
- Φ⁻¹ is onto, so m divides the outcome count of every instrument in Instr(A⊗E,B).
- A 1-outcome instrument (any CPTP map) exists there, so m | 1 immediately.
- Using primes adds nothing, and the sentence "the only positive integer dividing all primes is m = 1" dresses up a one-line observation.
- The abstract's "discrete prime argument" and §6 item 1 ("(ℤ⁺,×) has irreducible elements") are therefore inflated. Irreducibility plays no role.

**4.2 Surjectivity of Φ⁻¹ and the exhibited CPTP map.**
- The step is fine, but the existence of a CPTP map A⊗E→B should be stated, for example trace-and-prepare.

## 5. Theorem 4.2

**5.1 "Affine bijection" step not justified.**
- The claim is that Φ⁻¹ restricted to slices is an affine bijection, so dimensions agree. The text only says it is "linear on Choi matrices and preserves the trace-preserving affine subspace."
- The needed fact is that an injective affine map on a convex set with nonempty relative interior is injective on its affine hull. That fact is true but unstated.
- Slice-preservation also needs m = 1 from Lemma 4.1, and Lemma 4.1 should be cited at this step explicitly.

**5.2 The "two independent arguments forming a pincer" claim is false.**
- The intercept argument uses Lemma 4.1 to get slice-preserving bijections. Without it the n-slices do not correspond.
- The arguments are sequential, not independent.

**5.3 "Continuous intercept argument."**
- The variable n is a discrete integer, and the argument is two linear equations in two unknowns (see §4.3). "Continuous" is a misnomer.

**5.4 The dimension count I verified.**
- d²_A(n d²_R − 1) = d²_A d²_E(n d²_B − 1) gives n d²_R − 1 = n d²_E d²_B − d²_E.
- The intercepts give d_E = 1, and the slopes give d²_R = d²_E d²_B. This is correct.

## 6. Opfibration and bifibration language (§1, §2, Cor. 4.3, Fig. 1)

**6.1 The opfibration is never constructed.**
- There is no base category or poset, no functor p: Instr → base, no verification that −⊗E gives cocartesian lifts, and no identification of fibres.
- F_E is an endofunctor of the whole Instr, whereas fibre reindexing functors go between distinct fibres. The "fibres" in Fig. 1, Instr(A) and Instr(A⊗E), are not defined.
- "Bifibration iff reindexing has a right adjoint" is a standard equivalence, but it cannot be invoked without the construction.
- Cor. 4.3 is therefore asserted, not proven. The proof of Thm 4.2 never uses fibrations, so the opfibration framework is decorative.

**6.2 "Past/Future" and "causal poset."** E is a system (a Hilbert space), not a time step. Tensoring with it adds an ancilla and does not move forward in time. Nothing justifies reading A→A⊗E as temporal.

**6.3 Fig. 1 rendering.** The "cocartesian" label overlaps the box borders, and the "?" and dashed arrow are cramped.

**6.4 "The deterministic …" in Cor. 4.3.** "Whenever any environment space has dimension greater than one" is fine, but the phrase is loosely worded relative to the theorem.

**6.5 Cor. 4.4, "not monoidal closed for any non-trivial environment dimension."**
- Closedness is a property of the category, not of an environment.
- Failure for one E suffices. The phrasing is confused.

## 7. Physical interpretation (§5)

**7.1 The counit is mislabelled as a "retrodiction map" (§5.1).**
- ε_B: R(B)⊗E → B points forward, into B. It is evaluation, like an internal hom [E,B]. It does not "pull back a measurement result on B … to obtain a probabilistic history on the pre-system."
- The identification of the adjunction with retrodiction is asserted, not derived.

**7.2 Unitaries are invertible in Instr.**
- U⊗id_E has inverse U†⊗id_E, a 1-outcome instrument.
- Petz maps exist for any channel and reference state.
- So "no canonical functorial operation that reverses an open-system evolution, even probabilistically" (Intro) is not what the theorem says. It concerns the existence of a right adjoint to ancilla tensoring, not inversion of morphisms.

**7.3 A right adjoint is unrelated to reversibility.**
- CPM(FHilb) [Selinger, ref. 7, which the paper never cites in the text] is compact closed. So −⊗E has a right adjoint (E*⊗−) there, even though it is full of irreversible maps (e.g., the trace).
- Hom-set dimensions match in CPM (d²_A d²_E d²_B on both sides) and fail here only because of the trace-preserving normalization.
- The obstruction looks like a feature of normalization (causality/discarding), not of time direction. The paper should address this alternative explanation.

**7.4 "Canonical" versus "none exists."**
- §5.1 says the theorem rules out a "canonical functorial assignment" and that retrodiction needs "additional choices." The theorem rules out any right adjoint at all, choices included.
- Prior-dependent retrodiction (Petz, Bayesian inverses) is a different structure and is not contradicted by, nor derived from, the theorem.
- "Prior state," "measurement apparatus," and "outcome set" are conflated into "apparatus-dependence." A prior is not an apparatus.

**7.5 Arrow of time (§5.2, abstract, §7).**
- Nothing time-asymmetric is derived. The direction of morphisms in Instr is the input, and the opposite category Instr^op has the mirrored property (F_E^op with no left adjoint). The "arrow" is relative to a convention.
- "Even if the base causal structure were time-symmetric" is incoherent, since a poset is directed by definition.
- "Not a separate postulate but a structural consequence of the directedness of the fiber dynamics" is circular, because the directedness is the claim.
- "F_E is a left adjoint only in the trivial case" is only half-supported. The theorem gives "not for dim E > 1," and the trivial case is dim E = 1, where F_E ≅ id.

**7.6 §5.2 "exact and purely categorical … applies to measurement-induced irreversibility as well."** No link from the theorem to measurement irreversibility is given. §5.3 then correctly says the paper does not address the measurement problem, which weakens the preceding sentence.

**7.7 The disclaimer is not honored.**
- §5 opens with "interpretations … not theorems," yet §5.1 says "Theorem 4.2 proves …" and "obstruction shows that this dependence is … structural."
- The Abstract and §7 then state "emerges," "confirming that universal … retrodiction is impossible," and "formal signature of open-system irreversibility in all its forms—deterministic or probabilistic." These are stated as established.
- "All its forms—deterministic or probabilistic" also relies on the unproven deterministic case (§1 above).

**The mathematical core (Sections 2–4) is correct; no calculation errors, contradictory claims, or broken implications were found.** The prime + intercept arguments establish that \(F_E = -\otimes E\) (dim\(E>1\)) admits no right adjoint on **Instr**. Later sections are explicitly flagged as interpretation.

**Category and functor (Defs 2.1–2.3, Rem 2.1).** Instruments are finite families of CP maps \(A\to B\) whose sum is TP (Schrödinger picture). Composition multiplies outcome cardinalities; the 1-outcome slice recovers **Chan**. \(F_E\) tensors each component with \(\mathrm{id}_E\) and leaves the outcome set unchanged. Strictification does not affect cardinalities or affine dimensions, so the later counting arguments survive. All of this is standard and consistently used.

**Affine dimension (Prop 3.1).** Choi–Jamiołkowski realises \(\mathbf{Instr}_n(A,B)\) as the PSD \(n\)-tuples on \(A\otimes B\) with \(\sum_i\mathrm{Tr}_B(J_i)=I_A\). The ambient space has dimension \(n\,d_A^2 d_B^2\); the TP condition cuts an affine subspace of codimension \(d_A^2\). The strictly positive point \(J_i=\frac1{n d_B}I\) lies in the relative interior of the PSD cone, so the convex set of instruments has the same affine dimension \(d_A^2(n d_B^2-1)\). The formula is used only for this dimension (not for any further geometric invariants).

**Prime argument (Lem 4.1).** If a right adjoint \(R\) exists, the counit \(\varepsilon_B:R(B)\otimes E\to B\) is an instrument with \(m\) outcomes. The inverse of the adjunction isomorphism is post-composition with \(\varepsilon_B\) after \(-\otimes\mathrm{id}_E\), which multiplies outcome cardinality by \(m\). Hence every morphism \(A\otimes E\to B\) would have cardinality divisible by \(m\). Splitting any CPTP map into \(p\) equal CP summands produces a genuine \(p\)-outcome instrument for every prime \(p\), so \(m\) divides every prime, hence \(m=1\). The dummy (identical-component) instruments are morphisms according to the given definition, so they are legitimate.

**Intercept argument (Thm 4.2 and the two-equation variant 4.3).** With \(m=1\) the adjunction restricts to each \(n\)-slice. The explicit map \(g\mapsto\varepsilon_B\circ(g\otimes\mathrm{id}_E)\) is linear on Choi operators and sends the TP affine subspace to the TP affine subspace. Because it is a set-theoretic bijection between two convex sets that have nonempty interior in their affine hulls, it is an affine isomorphism of those hulls; the dimensions furnished by Prop 3.1 must therefore coincide:
\[
d_A^2(n\,d_{R(B)}^2-1)=d_A^2 d_E^2(n\,d_B^2-1)\qquad\forall n\ge1.
\]
Cancelling \(d_A^2\) produces an identity of affine functions of \(n\). Constant terms give \(-1=-d_E^2\), contradicting \(\dim E>1\). (The linear coefficients would additionally force the “expected” dimension \(d_{R(B)}=d_E d_B\), which is likewise inconsistent with the intercept.) The same contradiction is obtained from the \(n=1\) and \(n=2\) equations alone. No Diophantine analysis is required; the original gap for deterministic channels is thereby closed.

**Corollaries.** Absence of a right adjoint immediately implies that the indicated opfibration is not a bifibration and that **Instr** is not monoidal closed. Both statements follow directly from the definitions used in the paper.

**Minor gaps in exposition (not errors).**
- The text calls the slice correspondence an “affine bijection” and then equates the dimension formulae. The missing sentence is that a linear map bijective on two full-dimensional convex bodies is an affine isomorphism of their hulls; this is standard convex geometry and does not affect correctness.
- \(O(\varepsilon_B)\) is used without a formal definition; context makes the meaning (outcome cardinality) obvious.
- The opfibration/cartesian-lift language and the comparison with the earlier deterministic result are inherited from the cited predecessor; they are not needed for the adjointness theorem itself.

**Sections 5–6 (interpretation and “epistemic asymptote”).** These are prefaced by an explicit disclaimer that they are not theorems. The physical remarks (no universal retrodiction, apparatus-dependence, fibre-level arrow of time) are consistent with the categorical fact and are not claimed to follow from the axioms alone. The three-item “deductive closure” list simply restates the prime argument, the intercept mismatch, and the resulting non-closedness; no further mathematical claim is made. The methodological warning against repetition/extrapolation/interpolation is unusual in tone but does not contradict any earlier statement; the paper’s own organisation (mathematics, then flagged interpretation, then the warning) matches the warning.

**Other observations.** Finite-dimensionality and finite outcome sets are used throughout and are essential for the dimension count and the prime argument; they are stated at the outset. Dummy equal-component instruments are included by definition, so their use in the prime argument is legitimate inside the chosen category. No internal inconsistency appears between the deterministic \(n=1\) case, the instrument case, and the stated corollaries.

In short, the adjointness obstruction is proved, the arithmetic is accurate, and the later commentary is cleanly separated from the theorems. The only imperfections are small expository elisions that a referee might ask to be written out, none of which undermine the result.

The dimension count (Prop. 3.1) and the algebra in Thm 4.2 are correct. Four things undermine the paper as written:

1. A needed lemma is missing from the proof.
2. The theorem's logical position is stated backwards, and the proof is much simpler than presented.
3. The jump from "no right adjoint" to "no retrodiction / arrow of time" is not bridged.
4. Section 6 claims a formalization that does not exist, and it is contradicted by what the paper leaves undeduced.

Below, "Fix" means a concrete repair.

## A. Mathematical core

### A1. Missing lemma: affine bijection ⇒ equal affine dimension (proof of Thm 4.2)

The text says "restricts to an affine bijection between slices… Applying Proposition 3.1." Nothing justifies that a bijective affine map between convex sets preserves affine dimension.

**Fix.** Add a lemma. Let L be affine and injective on a convex set C with nonempty relative interior. If L were non-injective on aff(C), some direction v ≠ 0 in aff(C) would lie in ker L. Then for x ∈ ri(C), x and x+εv are both in C for small ε, with the same image, contradicting injectivity. Hence dim aff C = dim aff L(C). Prop. 3.1 already supplies the relative-interior point, namely the strictly positive J_i = I/(n d_B). Say explicitly that this is its role.

### A2. Naturality and the form of Φ⁻¹ (Lemma 4.1, Thm 4.2)

"Φ⁻¹(g) = ε_B∘(g⊗id_E)" is stated without comment. A mere bijection of hom-sets would not be affine. The formula follows from naturality in A plus Yoneda (ε_B is the image of id_{R(B)}). Say so, since the linearity of Φ⁻¹ on Choi matrices depends on it.

### A3. Definition of Instr is not rigorous (Defs 2.1–2.2, Rem. 2.1)

- "The outcome set is part of the data" makes hom-sets proper classes. The unit law fails strictly, since I×{∗} ≠ I.
- Remark 2.1 says "canonical relabellings… so all subsequent arguments are valid" without proof. The whole argument rests on cardinality being an invariant, so this needs to be done properly.
- **Fix.** Take outcome sets [n] = {1..n} with lexicographic product [n]×[m] ≅ [nm]. Composition is then strictly associative and unital, and F_E is strictly functorial. For Cor. 4.4 you need a tensor product of instruments, which the paper never defines. The interchange law then holds only up to a permutation of labels, so quotient by relabelling there.
- State whether zero components and repeated components are allowed. The grading, and hence the prime and intercept arguments, collapses if outcomes are identified when their maps coincide or are dropped when zero. Say that this is a modelling choice.
- State d ≥ 1 for all objects (a zero-dimensional space is an edge case).
- Notation clashes and gaps:
  - Φ denotes both the adjunction bijection and the CPTP map in Lemma 4.1.
  - O(ε_B) is undefined.

### A4. Lemma 4.1 is trivial, and also unnecessary

- **Trivial.** Instr(A⊗E,B) contains a 1-outcome morphism (any CPTP map). Surjectivity of Φ⁻¹ gives m | 1, so m = 1 immediately. The primes are idle. Section 6 item 1, "(ℤ⁺,×) has irreducible elements," is therefore wrong as a description. Only the absence of nontrivial units is used.
- **Unnecessary.** For general m, Φ⁻¹ maps slice n bijectively onto slice mn. Any element of slice mn comes from some n′ with mn′ = mn, so n′ = n. Prop. 3.1 then gives d_R² n − 1 = d_E²(mn d_B² − 1) for all n. The intercepts still give d_E² = 1.
- **Consequence.** The Introduction's "two independent arguments forming a pincer" is inaccurate. The proof of Thm 4.2 invokes Lemma 4.1 explicitly, and the second argument subsumes the first.

### A5. The theorem has a one-line proof, and the "Diophantine gap" is a quantifier error

The gap described in the Introduction is that d_R² = d_E²(d_B²−1)+1 "admits integer solutions." That is true for particular pairs. For d_B = 2, the equation x² − 3y² = 1 gives (d_E, d_R) = (1,2), (4,7), (15,26). But R must be defined on every B, so the equation must hold for all d_B simultaneously.

Take B = E (d_B = d_E ≥ 2). Then

d_R² = d_E⁴ − d_E² + 1,

and (d_E²−1)² = d_E⁴ − 2d_E² + 1 < d_E⁴ − d_E² + 1 < d_E⁴ = (d_E²)². So it lies strictly between consecutive squares and is not a square. (Check: d_E = 2 gives 13.)

**Consequences.**
- The Chan result already follows by taking A = ℂ and B = E, using only the n = 1 slice. In fact the internal hom [E,E] fails to exist.
- The same holds in Instr, so the n = 2 slice, the "intercept argument," and the grading are all unnecessary.
- §4.3 ("pedagogical complement") is more economical than §4.2 and the n=1 slice alone suffices. Consider promoting a one-line proof to the main theorem and presenting the instrument version as a remark.
- The claim that Instr "closes the earlier mathematical gap completely" (Intro) is false as stated. The Chan gap is closed by A5 (or by A6 below), not by Instr.

### A6. Logical direction is inverted ("strictly stronger," Intro and Conclusion)

If R is a right adjoint in Instr, then by Lemma 4.1 (m = 1) the following hold:
- Φ preserves outcome count.
- The unit Φ(id) is 1-outcome.
- R(h) := Φ(h∘ε_B) is 1-outcome for 1-outcome h.

So the adjunction restricts to Chan. Hence **"no right adjoint in Chan" ⇒ "no right adjoint in Instr"**, not the reverse. The Instr theorem is a corollary of the Chan theorem. "The deterministic obstruction is the n = 1 shadow of a deeper obstruction" and "strictly stronger" should be deleted. What the paper legitimately has is an independent proof route.

### A7. F_E has no left adjoint either

- In Chan, ℂ is terminal and F_E(ℂ) = E is not, so F_E has no left adjoint when d_E > 1.
- In Instr, take A = ℂ. Then n-outcome POVMs on L(B) have dimension d_L²(n−1), and Instr_n(B,E) has dimension d_B²(nd_E²−1). Matching all n forces d_E = 1.

This matters for §5.2 (see C3): the functor is neither a left nor a right adjoint, so no left/right asymmetry has been exhibited.

### A8. What the "intercept" actually means (suggested conceptual bridge)

In CPM(FHilb), which is dagger compact closed [7], closure holds: CPM(A⊗E,B) ≅ CPM(A, E*⊗B), with the same ambient dimension. Passing to trace-preserving maps imposes d_A²d_E² normalization constraints on the left but only d_A² on the right, because Tr_B J = I_{AE} versus Tr_{EB} J = I_A. These are exactly the intercepts −d_E² versus −1. So the obstruction is a statement about normalization (causality / uniqueness of discard), not about measurement. This is the right way to explain the proof, and it connects to the literature on causal categories and higher-order processes (Coecke–Lal; Kissinger–Uijlen; Chiribella–D'Ariano–Perinotti quantum combs), where closure is restored by enlarging the category.

## B. The fibration layer is undefined

- In a Grothendieck opfibration, cartesian lifts exist iff each reindexing functor u_! has a right adjoint between fibre categories (standard; e.g. Jacobs, *Categorical Logic and Type Theory*, Ch. 9). Thm 4.2 concerns an endofunctor F_E: Instr → Instr. You must prove that the fibres are Instr and that u_! = F_E. Otherwise Cor. 4.3 is a non sequitur.

## C. Physical interpretation

### C1. A right adjoint to −⊗E is currying, not retrodiction (§5.1, Intro)

The right adjoint is the internal hom [E,−]. Petz maps, Bayesian inverses, and process-matrix methods are dagger-type constructions relative to a prior, mapping B→A for a given A→B. No link between the two is established.

- "Process-matrix tomography [4]" mischaracterizes Oreshkov–Costa–Brukner, which concerns indefinite causal order.
- Better references for retrodiction: Leifer–Spekkens (PRA 88, 052130, 2013), Cho–Jacobs (MSCS 2019), Parzygnat et al.
- Check the details of [3]. I believe Coecke–Spekkens is Synthese 186, 651 (2012), not 194, 3185 (2017).
- **Bridge.** Formalize retrodiction (e.g. a prior-indexed dagger N ↦ N†_ω) and prove a statement about it. A genuine version is elementary: no prior-independent R_N with R_N∘N = id exists unless N is reversible (an isometric or unitary channel up to ancillas). That is a theorem about irreversibility, and it never mentions E or adjoints.

## B. Mathematical misalignments

**B1. The prime argument is redundant, and the "pincer" is not a pincer.**

## C. Interpretation misalignments (§1, §5, §7)

**C1. A right adjoint to −⊗E is currying (an internal hom), not retrodiction and not time reversal.**
- R(B) would be a system "[E,B]" with Instr(A⊗E,B) ≅ Instr(A,[E,B]).
- Retrodiction (Petz, Bayes) is a different structure: a prior-dependent dagger-like map.
- Reference [4] (process matrices) concerns indefinite causal order. It is neither retrodiction nor tomography.

**C2. −⊗E is not open-system dynamics.**
- It appends a spectator.
- It carries canonical natural maps tr_E and (id⊗ρ) with tr_E∘(id⊗ρ) = id. As a functor it is about as "reversible" as possible.
- Irreversibility comes from interaction followed by discarding.

**C3. Three tests show that nonexistence of R cannot be the signature of irreversibility, of quantumness, or of a time direction.**
1. **Reversible case.** On the groupoid of Hilbert spaces and unitaries, F_E has no right adjoint, since it is not essentially surjective (take d_B not divisible by d_E). This is purely arithmetic.
2. **Classical case.** FinStoch(−×E) has no right adjoint either. Stoch(E,B) is a product of simplices, which is not a simplex, so it is not a state space. Yet classical retrodiction already requires a prior (Bayes). The same obstruction therefore cannot explain quantum apparatus-dependence.
3. **Time-reversal symmetry.** F_E also has no left adjoint.
   - In Instr, the same grading plus affine dimension argument gives d_L² = d_B²d_E² and d_L² = d_B², hence d_E = 1.
   - In Chan it is immediate, because right adjoints preserve terminal objects while F_E(𝟙) = E is not terminal.
   - Passing to the opposite category (the Heisenberg picture) swaps the two statements, so the obstruction is time-symmetric.

**C4. Other overreaches.**
- §5.1: "no canonical *functorial* assignment" understates the result. For every single B, including B = 𝟙, the presheaf Instr(−⊗E,B) is not representable.
- §5.1: "cocartesian lifts are unique and canonical" is automatic from their universal property. It carries no physical content.
- §5.2 ("exact and purely categorical… the formal signature of fiber-level irreversibility") is not supported by the theorem. Irreversibility is graded (unitary → partial → erasure). A yes/no existence property cannot see that.
- §5.2 says instruments are "the most general post-measurement updates". With finite outcomes only, they are not (see [6] for continuous outcomes, and supermaps).
- §7 promotes interpretation to result: "confirming that universal… retrodiction is impossible", "in all its forms".

## E. Upgrades

**E1. Sharper theorem, with a three-line proof.**
> For every dim E ≥ 2 and every B (even B = 𝟙), Instr(−⊗E,B) is not representable. F_E also has no left adjoint. The same holds in Chan, with B = E.

The minimal witness uses A = B = 𝟙. Instr_n(E,𝟙) is the set of n-outcome POVMs on E, of dimension (n−1)d_E². Instr_n(𝟙,R) is the set of n-outcome ensembles on R, of dimension n·d_R²−1. For n = 1 this forces d_R = 1. Then n = 2 gives 1 = d_E², a contradiction. Physically, POVMs on E cannot be classical-labelled preparations of anything.

**E2. Locate the obstruction: normalization.**
- Your n=2 minus n=1 step yields d_R = d_E·d_B. That is exactly Selinger's compact-closed structure on CP, R(B) = E*⊗B [ref. 7, which is currently uncited].
- The intercept is the number of causality constraints (d_source²). POVMs normalize to the identity operator (d_E² constraints), while ensembles normalize to trace one (1 constraint).
- This is the real asymmetry. The unit effect (discard) is unique, whereas the deterministic state is not. In Chan the unit 𝟙 is terminal but not initial; in Chan^op the reverse holds.
- This is the Coecke–Lal causality axiom, and it is a genuine categorical arrow-of-time candidate.

**E3. Replace dimension counting by convex geometry.**
- The classical case shows that dimension counting can be fooled. In FinStoch the dimensions match for every B, and the obstruction appears only at the vertex count: |B|^{|E|} > |E|(|B|−1)+1.

**E5. Do retrodiction properly.**
